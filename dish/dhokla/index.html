Dhokla

A soft, tangy steamed cake of fermented rice and gram-flour batter, cut into squares and finished with mustard seeds. Gujarat eats it for breakfast, with tea and at weddings.

Gujarat · snack

History

11th century · Folk tale (traditional)

Popular accounts say an 11th-century Jain text mentions dukkia, a steamed dish of pulses, and call it dhokla's ancestor. They give different years (1066 or 1068 CE) and don't name the text, so we can't confirm it yet.

c. 1520 · Cleared (documented)

The word dhokla appears in Varnaka Samuccaya, a Gujarati text dated to around 1520 CE. It is the earliest written use of the name we know of.

classic method · Cleared (documented)

K. T. Achaya records the classic method: two parts rice to one part gram flour, soured overnight with curd, steamed in a tray, then cut and finished with fried mustard seeds, fresh coriander and grated coconut.

khaman · Unknown

Who first made khaman, the all-gram-flour cousin raised with soda instead of a long ferment, is not recorded in the sources we checked.

Ingredients

Rice, Chickpea (gram, besan), Milk, curd, ghee and khoya, Mustard (seeds and greens), Coconut, Chilli
